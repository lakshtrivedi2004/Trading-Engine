How the models work (and why you can trust the accuracy numbers)
  • Direction: regularised logistic regression on information available at the close — the index's 1/5/20-day returns, 20-day volatility, distance from its 200-day average, day range, India VIX change and the US session that closed before the Indian close. The evening US session is not used: it hasn't happened when the forecast is made.
  • Move size: HAR model (Corsi 2009) — the next session's absolute move from the last day's, week's and month's average absolute moves plus the day's high-low range. Volatility clusters, which is why this part is forecastable.
  • Walk-forward: the models are refit every month using only data that existed at that time; every scored forecast was made without seeing its outcome.
  • Baselines and test: direction vs "past share of up days"; move size vs "last 20 days' average". The Diebold-Mariano test (1995, Newey-West errors) decides: |t| below 2 means no better than the baseline.
